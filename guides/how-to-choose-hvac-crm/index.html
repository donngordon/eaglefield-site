Buyer's guide

How to choose an HVAC CRM.

Choosing HVAC software is a multi-year decision. This checklist covers what to look for, what it really costs, and the questions to ask in every demo, whichever product you pick.

1. Start with your biggest leak

Write down where jobs go wrong today: wrong information at dispatch, missing photos and notes, callbacks, parts runs, unpaid invoices or lost maintenance renewals. Judge every product on how it fixes those first.

2. Must-have features for HVAC

  • Customer and equipment records with model, serial and service history
  • Scheduling and dispatch board
  • A technician mobile app that works with gloves on and poor signal
  • Pricebook, quotes, invoices and payments in the field
  • Maintenance agreements and member pricing
  • Customer notifications, including on-my-way tracking
  • Reports on revenue, callbacks and technician performance

3. Know the real cost

Ask for the full monthly price at your team size, plus setup fees, add-ons, payment processing rates, contract length and the price at renewal. Some vendors publish prices; others quote only.

4. Check the contract and your data

Can you leave month to month? Can you export customers, jobs and invoices whenever you want, in a standard format?

5. Questions to ask in every demo

  1. Show me a call becoming a job. How do you make sure the tech gets the right information?
  2. What stops a technician from closing a job with no photos or notes?
  3. How does a tech find a part he doesn't have on the van?
  4. What does the customer see between booking and arrival?
  5. What will I pay in month 13?

6. Run a real pilot

Put two technicians on it for two weeks with real jobs before you switch the whole shop.

FAQ

Common questions.

What is the most important feature in an HVAC CRM?

It depends on where your business loses money. For most HVAC shops it is dispatch accuracy and making sure every job is closed with complete photos and notes.

How long does it take to switch HVAC software?

With a clean data export, a small or mid-size shop can switch in one to three weeks, including training.

Should I sign a multi-year contract?

Only if the discount is worth giving up flexibility. Many owners prefer month-to-month software with a locked price.
